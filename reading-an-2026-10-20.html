The banyan and the bypass

Test ID: Topic: Editorial difficulty: (a guess before reading, not a measured level)
Words: Reading time: about 2 minutes
Source: original passage written for practice, so full text is included.

The town of Kherwadi grew around its bus stand, and the bus stand grew around a banyan. The tree is older than the road, older than most of the shops, and wide enough for three auto-rickshaws to wait in its shade. Now the district wants to widen the road into a bypass, and the banyan stands exactly where the new lane must go. The council has proposed moving the tree rather than cutting it down.

Meena D'Souza, the engineer in charge, believes the move can succeed. At a public meeting she presented a modest file: records of three roadside banyans in neighbouring districts that were dug up, trucked to new pits and are alive today. 'The method works,' she said. Her team would trim the roots over two months and water the new pit through two summers. She promised careful work, not miracles.

Raghunath Kibe, a retired forest officer who walks to the bus stand every morning, is unconvinced. The trees in her file, he points out, were young; no one in it moved a giant whose trunk four men cannot circle. He speaks of the owlets nesting in the hollow near the first fork, and of mango crates that stay cool under the canopy through May. 'A moved tree is a planted tree,' he says. 'You can plant the log, but not the life around it.'

The quarrel has split the town curiously. Nearly everyone wants the road, and nearly everyone wants the tree; the dispute is over what should count as proof. D'Souza trusts her file because it can be measured. Kibe trusts his mornings. The school has joined in: students keep a weekly diary of the banyan, counting nests and pacing out the shade at noon, so the council's decision, due next month, might rest on something observed under this tree and not only reported from elsewhere.

Whatever the council decides, the banyan has already changed how Kherwadi argues: at the last meeting, both sides quoted the students' diary.
