The bed left off the screen

Test ID: Topic: Editorial difficulty: (a guess before reading, not a measured level)
Words: Reading time: about 2 minutes
Source: original passage written for practice, so full text is included.

At one corner of the housing society's terrace garden, Devika kept a narrow bed without a moisture sensor. The other beds sent readings to a shared screen near the stairwell. Arun called the unconnected bed a blind spot and offered to buy another sensor. Devika refused. She called it the barefoot bed, not because numbers were false, but because numbers could make attention narrow.

Each morning she pressed two fingers into the soil, lifted the edge of a pot and smelled the leaves before looking at the screen. The habit sometimes caught what the readings missed. After one night of drizzle, the screen said the beds were adequately wet, but Devika's fingers found that only the surface was damp and the lower layer remained dry. At another time, the sensor caught a slow leak beneath the mulch before her hand or eye noticed it. She treated neither result as a victory.

When the society's water use fell that month, Devika told Arun that the barefoot bed had taught everyone to water less. Arun was not persuaded. The society had also begun mulching the beds and had repaired a dripping hose in the same week. A neighbouring garden with no unmeasured bed had reduced its use by about the same amount after making those changes. The barefoot bed might have helped, he said, but it had not worked alone.

At the end of the season, Devika still did not install the sensor. On the bed's wooden tag, she painted a blue line each time a hand check contradicted the screen and a gray line each time the screen corrected a hand. By winter the tag held both colours. She left space for more. The bed stayed outside the system, not as proof that touch was superior to measurement, but as a place where each way of noticing had to answer to the other.
