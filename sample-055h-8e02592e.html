The ticket in the interview folder

Test ID: Topic: Editorial difficulty: (a guess before reading, not a measured level)
Words: Reading time: about 2 minutes
Source: original passage written for practice, so full text is included.

In Nera Sen's invented story, a child watches her father rehearse answers before a mirror each evening. She believes he is preparing for a play. The reader is told at once that he has a job interview, after months without work. Yet the story never replaces the child's explanation with an adult one. Its difficulty lies less in discovering what the rehearsal means than in deciding what to do with the child's mistake.

One possible revision would give the father a speech about unemployment. That speech would settle the rehearsal's meaning, but it would also change the reader's position. We would stand beside the informed adult and look back at the mistaken child. Without it, we must understand the father's anxiety while remaining inside a consciousness that cannot name it. The child's account is partial, but it is not emotionally empty: she notices that his voice shrinks whenever he reaches the question about his last job.

This is not an argument that obscurity makes fiction serious. If Sen withheld every clue about the interview, the rehearsal might remain a puzzle with several equally unsupported solutions. The opening disclosure gives the reader enough ground for an interpretation. The narrative is reticent about explanation, not about evidence. What remains unstated is how the father feels, not what appointment he is preparing for.

Nor does the child's mistake simply disappear once we understand more than she does. She makes a paper ticket and applauds his next answer. To the father, that applause may offer an assurance of worth she does not know he needs. An error about the facts becomes an accurate response to a need. Calling her merely unreliable would miss that distinction.

Imagine ending with the father explaining that there is no play. Now imagine him slipping her ticket into his interview folder. The second ending does not make the child better informed. It allows her mistaken account and his knowledge to travel together, without requiring either to become the other's language.
