A courtroom after the chairs move

Test ID: Topic: Editorial difficulty: (a guess before reading, not a measured level)
Words: Reading time: about 2 minutes
Source: original fictional passage written for practice, so full text is included.

An imagined town has two identical neighbouring halls. One is used every morning as a courtroom and every evening for dance lessons. The other has stood empty for a year. A visiting photographer makes careful images of their floors, walls and windows. From those images, a viewer cannot tell which room has hosted judgments, rehearsals or nothing at all.

The failure does not show that the photographs are inaccurate. They may record every visible feature the photographer intended to capture. What they do not record is a room's use through time. A courtroom is not simply a room with the right arrangement of wood and plaster. Its role depends on people authorised to act there, procedures they follow and occasions when those procedures are actually carried out.

The evening dancers do not need to rebuild the walls before the room serves their purpose. They move the chairs and begin a lesson. Nor does a cancelled hearing turn the hall into a place that has never been a courtroom. A continuing role can survive an interval in which it is not exercised. The relation between a place and its use therefore cannot be read directly from each physical arrangement.

Yet the role is not detached from physical conditions. If the roof falls in, calling the ruin a courtroom will not supply a safe place for a hearing. Repairs may be needed before the role can be exercised again. The point is that a description of the structure and a description of its role answer different questions. Neither can simply replace the other, even though the role depends on having some suitable structure.

The photographer returns with another plan. She makes a sequence: chairs being arranged, a clerk opening a file, people waiting outside, and later a teacher clearing space for dancers. The empty hall is photographed at matching hours. These images still cannot certify anyone's legal authority, but they make the contrast she missed visible as a pattern of activity. Instead of seeking the courtroom in the grain of a tabletop, she begins to look for what people do around it.
