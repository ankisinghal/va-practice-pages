The age beside the evidence

Test ID: Topic: Editorial difficulty: (a guess before reading, not a measured level)
Words: Reading time: about 2 minutes
Source: original fictional passage written for practice, so full text is included.

In an imagined costume workshop, Rupa opens a locked cupboard to inspect six silk coats. There are no holes in any of them. Her assistant suggests using the same untreated silk for a theatre's outdoor banners: the cloth has survived ten years, so it must be exceptionally durable. Rupa refuses to treat the unmarked coats as an answer to the banner question.

The cupboard has been dark and dry throughout those years, and no one has worn the coats. That history tells Rupa something useful about storage. It tells her little about how the silk would respond to rain, sunlight or repeated pulling. A thing can reach an impressive age without encountering the conditions under which another thing is expected to last.

The assistant then points to a coat hanging near the window. Its colour is faded, and he takes this as stronger evidence: here is visible damage, unlike the suspicious perfection inside the cupboard. But visible damage does not automatically make a comparison relevant. The window coat has met sunlight, yet it has never hung outdoors or been fastened under tension. Its fading cannot establish how a banner's seams would behave in wind.

Rupa does not conclude that old objects teach nothing or that every new use requires starting from ignorance. An old sailing flag made from the same silk, repeatedly hoisted in weather like the theatre's, could provide a useful comparison. Even then, differences in stitching and care would matter. She wants a history of the relevant demands, not an object chosen merely because its survival or damage is easy to see.

The workshop's inventory therefore gains a second column beside each item's age: what it has actually endured. For the cupboard coats, Rupa writes only 'dark, dry storage'. She leaves their suitability for outdoor banners undecided. The empty verdict is not a criticism of the cloth. It is a refusal to make ten quiet years answer a question those years never asked. Before calling the silk proven, the assistant must say what it has been proved against.
