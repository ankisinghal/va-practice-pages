The dome that slowed in the rain

Words: Reading time: about 2 minutes
Source: original passage written for practice, so full text is included.

The dome of the Khetri observatory had turned smoothly for forty years, driven by a motor older than anyone in the village below. That monsoon, the motor began to strain on some nights, slowing the dome to a crawl until the telescope lost its target. Bhanu, the caretaker, said the cause was humidity. The strain came only on damp nights, he pointed out, when moisture swelled the wooden track the dome rode on; through every dry week of the summer he had oiled the track without one bad night, until the rains began.

Saira, the young astronomer who had joined in the spring, doubted that reading. The southern surveys were the only nights the dome was ever wound through its full arc, the heaviest work the motor did, and when she climbed up to look she found the new lubricant, thin and expensive, pooled at the southern end of the track, the wood beneath it nearly bare. If the strain belonged to the heaviest winding rather than the damp air, she said, the logbook would read exactly as it did.

Each could point to the logbook. Bhanu's pages showed the strain marked against nights of high humidity. Saira's margin notes showed that those same nights were, without exception, southern-survey nights. The monsoon had been unusually wet that year, and the southern survey had begun in the very same month, so the two explanations had never once been apart.

They agreed the logbook could not settle the matter, because the damp nights and the southern surveys had arrived together. For a fortnight they would alternate: southern fields on dry nights, northern fields on damp ones, with every turn of the dome timed and noted. Bhanu entered the plan in the logbook as "the experiment", and Saira ordered a thicker grease from the city, to be kept unopened until the fortnight was done.
