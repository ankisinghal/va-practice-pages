The question beside the photograph

Test ID: Topic: Editorial difficulty: (a guess before reading, not a measured level)
Words: Reading time: about 2 minutes
Source: original fictional passage written for practice, so full text is included.

In a story, Leela visits her father's house to persuade him to move into a smaller flat. She brings photographs of bright rooms and a list of nearby shops. He answers her questions about stairs, rent and buses. Whenever she asks whether he likes a particular flat, however, he asks which way its balcony faces. She has already sent him the floor plans.

The story does not explain his interest in the balcony. It follows Leela as she measures doorways, sorts old bills and calculates removal costs. She interprets her father's repeated question as another obstacle to a sensible decision. Because readers receive events through her concerns, they too may initially treat the balcony as a minor detail holding up the real business. Her practical competence gives her judgments an authority the narration never grants them.

During lunch, her father turns the pots on his balcony towards the sun. He runs a finger along an old planting label, then returns it to the soil without speaking. Leela notices, but the story does not report a conclusion forming in her mind. Instead, she asks whether he still has the measurements of the new flat. The conversation appears to resume where it stopped, though the reader now has a reason to hear the question about the balcony differently.

It would be too quick to turn the scene into proof that moving is cruel or that Leela cares only about money. Her father has trouble climbing the stairs, and she has spent her holiday arranging visits. The practical problem remains real. What changes is the adequacy of her original description of it. A suitable home may require more than the features her list can record.

Before leaving, Leela crosses out the balcony width on one photograph. She does not replace it with a larger number. She writes a question to ask at the next visit: could the old pots come too? The story ends before the move is settled. Her new question does not solve the conflict; it changes what she is prepared to count as part of it.
