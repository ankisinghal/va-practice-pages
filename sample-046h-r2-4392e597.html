The song with more than one ending

Test ID: Topic: Editorial difficulty: (a guess before reading, not a measured level)
Words: Reading time: about 2 minutes
Source: original passage written for practice, so full text is included.

When a school in a desert town decided to revive an old harvest song for its winter programme, the music teacher, Zoya, began by recording the oldest singer in the neighbourhood. The students assumed the recording would settle the song. Then three elders offered three endings. One ended with the carts leaving before dawn; one lingered on the women mending the grain sacks; one stopped on a single held note. No singer called the others wrong.

Zoya invited a visiting musician to transcribe one performance. His page was precise, but it captured only the notes he had chosen to record. It omitted the singer's spoken aside - "Sing it as if the carts are still outside" - and the pause in which the audience usually answered. Zoya did not discard the transcription. She placed it beside a notebook in which students wrote every variation they heard, including memories that contradicted one another.

After a month, attendance at the after-school practice had risen. Zoya told the principal that the variation notebook had caused the rise because students wanted their own memories recognised. The principal asked for restraint. The school had also moved practice from the hot afternoon to early evening, and another section practising a different song without a notebook had seen attendance climb after the same change. The notebook may have mattered, she said, but it was not the only new thing.

Naina, a student, added her grandmother's spoken aside to the notebook after asking whether words outside the melody counted. Zoya told her to place them beside the tune. On the winter evening, the choir sang two of the recorded endings one after another. Before the final bar, Zoya lowered her hands and left the music unresolved for a breath. The audience filled the silence with the version it knew. The programme note called it one song, but the performance made clear that preserving it meant preserving the ways people continued to finish it.
