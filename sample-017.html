The coach and the table

Test ID: Topic: Editorial difficulty: (a guess before reading, not a measured level)
Words: Reading time: about 2 minutes
Source: original passage written for practice, so full text is included.

At the end of the season, a village football club must decide whether to keep its long-serving coach. Under her, the team has finished mid-table for six years. Some supporters call this steady and respectable. Others say a club that never improves is slowly falling behind.

The coach's defenders point out that the club has almost no money. Players are volunteers, the pitch floods in winter, and the best youngsters leave for bigger clubs. A coach who keeps such a team competitive, they argue, deserves credit that the league table cannot show.

Critics reply that excuses are easy to find. They note that two similar clubs nearby have risen steadily with comparable budgets. Yet neither side has examined how those clubs were run, so the comparison proves less than it seems to.

The committee eventually asks both groups to list what they would count as progress. The lists overlap less than expected. Some want trophies, while others want a stable squad and a safe pitch. The committee realises that the disagreement concerns goals more than the coach.
