The tank and the timetable

Test ID: Topic: Editorial difficulty: (a guess before reading, not a measured level)
Words: Reading time: about 2 minutes
Source: original passage written for practice, so full text is included.

A community garden on the edge of a city shares one water tank between thirty plots. For years, gardeners took what they needed, and the arrangement worked because everyone knew everyone. When a dry summer arrives, the tank empties before the end of the week, and neighbours begin to suspect one another.

The committee proposes a timetable that gives each plot a fixed slot. Some members welcome it as fair. Others object that a tiny herb bed and a large vegetable patch should not receive the same share. One elderly member adds that the timetable also removes the casual chats that used to happen at the tank.

After several meetings, the committee adopts slots that vary with plot size, and it asks each gardener to note when they collect water. The notes are not meant as punishment. They simply let everyone see how much is used, so that arguments can rest on records rather than guesses.

By autumn the tank still runs low in dry weeks, but the quarrels have faded. Fairness, the committee concludes, depends less on strict rules than on a shared understanding of how the resource is used.
