The last ten minutes

Test ID: Topic: Editorial difficulty: (a guess before reading, not a measured level)
Words: Reading time: about 2 minutes
Source: original passage written for practice, so full text is included.

Commentators love to say that a match is decided in the final minutes. Fans remember the dramatic goal or the missed free throw, and the story seems simple: one player rose to the pressure and another failed. But those last minutes often look decisive only because the earlier ones are forgotten.

Consider a team that leads by one point. Every decision now appears enormous. Should the coach protect the lead or risk another attack? Yet the same decision made in the first half would seem routine. The situation has not changed in kind; it has changed in how much attention it receives. A mistake that costs a goal at the end is remembered; an equal mistake in the opening minutes is soon lost.

This does not mean pressure is imaginary. Players do report faster heartbeats and narrower thoughts when the score is close, and some seem to handle this better than others. But it is hard to separate skill under pressure from plain luck, because the number of truly decisive moments in a season is small. A player who succeeds in three of them may be praised as "clutch"; one who fails in three may be blamed, though the difference could be a single bounce of the ball.

The sensible view, then, is neither to dismiss late-game heroics nor to treat them as proof of character. Final minutes deserve admiration for their drama, and caution about what they prove.
