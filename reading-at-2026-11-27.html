The weeks above both columns

Test ID: Topic: Editorial difficulty: (a guess before reading, not a measured level)
Words: Reading time: about 2 minutes
Source: original fictional passage written for practice, so full text is included.

In an imagined pottery workshop, Nila has spent three weeks making a set of tall vases for a hotel. Before they are fired, the hotel cancels its order. No deposit was paid. The clay can still be reused, but firing the vases would require another full day of fuel and work. Her partner suggests reclaiming the clay. Nila replies that doing so would waste the three weeks already spent.

The partner does not deny that those weeks were costly. He writes their cost on a sheet, then places it beside two columns headed finish and reclaim. The same figure belongs beneath both headings. Neither choice can give the weeks back. They need to compare what happens from this morning onwards, rather than award the earlier effort to whichever choice feels more respectful.

Finishing might nevertheless be sensible. A shop could buy the vases, or Nila might learn a firing technique that she expects to use again. Such benefits would belong in the finish column, alongside the additional fuel and work. Reclaiming would leave usable clay and free the kiln for another order. The partner asks for estimates of these consequences. He has not proved that reclaiming must win.

Nila objects that persistence matters in a workshop. Her partner agrees: customers need dependable makers, and abandoning work casually can damage trust. But this particular hotel has cancelled, and no other customer has been promised these vases. If completing them would protect a genuine future relationship, that would be another consequence to count. Calling the past effort a promise does not create a customer who is still waiting.

They leave the earlier cost written across the top of the sheet. Erasing it would make their accounts false. Beneath it, they compare two possible uses of tomorrow. Nila may still choose to fire the vases. What she can no longer say is that finishing will recover the weeks: those weeks explain how she reached this decision, not which remaining choice is better.
