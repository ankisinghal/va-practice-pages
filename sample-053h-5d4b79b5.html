The bus stand and the bulbs

Test ID: Topic: Editorial difficulty: (a guess before reading, not a measured level)
Words: Reading time: about 2 minutes
Source: original passage written for practice, so full text is included.

When the market streets of Salur posted a handsome year of sales, the town's traders' association knew exactly whom to thank: itself. In spring it paid for decorative lighting along six lanes and persuaded shops to open two hours later; by winter the tills were ringing as they had not in a decade. Its newsletter called the result "proof that ambience pays", and it asked members to fund a second, larger phase.

The timing, though, has a rival author. Salur's bus stand moved the same month the lights went up, from the bypass to a yard two hundred metres from the market's eastern gate. Buses from the surrounding villages now arrive hourly, and their passengers cross the market on foot. A bus stand looks like a small thing on a map, but each arrival turns dozens of people loose past shopfronts they once ignored in favour of the bypass.

The sales pattern fits the bus stand better than the bulbs. Receipts have risen most on the days the village buses run fullest, and the biggest gains are reported on the short stretch between the stand and the market gate. Lanes that glow with new lamps but lie away from that walking route have gained little more than the town average.

None of this makes the lighting worthless. Shopkeepers report more browsing after dark and a visibly thicker evening crowd, deliveries go more smoothly on lit lanes, and nobody misses the dark. The lights may even have helped the new visitors linger and spend a little more. But helping a crowd spend is not the same as producing the crowd, and the evidence says the crowd arrived by bus.

The distinction is worth money. If the first phase rode on the bus stand's footfall, more lights on remoter lanes may add costs without adding shoppers. The association can still choose the lights; a brighter market is a pleasant thing to buy. It should budget them as an amenity the town enjoys, not as a proven engine of sales, because so far the engine has been a bus stand, not a bulb.
