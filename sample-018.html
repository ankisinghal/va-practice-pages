The locked cabinet

Test ID: Topic: Editorial difficulty: (a guess before reading, not a measured level)
Words: Reading time: about 2 minutes
Source: original passage written for practice, so full text is included.

A school library has long kept its most valuable books in a locked cabinet. Students may look at them only if a teacher is present. The librarian has noticed that the cabinet is rarely opened, and she begins to wonder whether the books are protected or simply forgotten.

She proposes moving a few titles to an open shelf with a note asking readers to handle them gently. Some teachers worry that one careless student could cause damage that cannot be repaired. Others reply that a book nobody reads has lost much of its purpose, however carefully it is stored.

The principal agrees to a trial. Only the least fragile books are moved, and each is slipped into a clear sleeve. After a term, two covers have loosened, but the books have been borrowed more often than any others in the library.

The librarian does not claim that the trial settles the question. She notes that loosened covers are a real cost and that the most delicate books remain in the cabinet. What the trial shows, she says, is that protection and use are both worth weighing.
