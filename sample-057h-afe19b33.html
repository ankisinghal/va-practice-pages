The date inside the wall

Test ID: Topic: Editorial difficulty: (a guess before reading, not a measured level)
Words: Reading time: about 2 minutes
Source: original passage written for practice, so full text is included.

In an imagined excavation at the fort of Belan, a copper token lies beneath a wall's unbroken original plaster. Here, finishing the wall means applying that plaster. The token bears the date 48 of the local calendar. For this exercise, its date is genuine, and the excavators establish that it was placed there before the plaster was applied. A newspaper announces that the wall was finished in year 48. The discovery is real within the story; the precision of the announcement is not earned.

The token rules out a completion date before year 48, but it does not make every later date impossible. Someone could have kept it for thirty years before leaving it in the wall. Calling that possibility fanciful would require evidence about this particular token's use, not a general dislike of untidy answers. A dated object carries its own age into a building; it does not automatically lend the building its birthday.

A second record narrows the interval. A survey drawing made in year 63 shows the same wall already finished, including a distinctive crooked corner and the pattern of its plaster. Assume the drawing is correctly dated and its identification secure. Now the completion must fall no earlier than 48 and no later than 63. The two records do different jobs: the token bars an earlier date, while the drawing bars a later one.

It would still be arbitrary to split the interval and announce year 55 or 56. Neither record makes its middle more likely than its edges. Nor would discovering ten more tokens dated 48 necessarily settle the matter: a single collection could have been stored and deposited together. More objects need not supply a different kind of constraint.

The historian's conclusion is therefore an interval, not an apology for failing to find a date. It marks the accounts the evidence excludes and the possibilities it leaves open. A later discovery may contract that interval. Until then, printing its boundaries is more informative than concealing them behind a precise numeral.
