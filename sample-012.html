The story told again

Test ID: Topic: Editorial difficulty: (a guess before reading, not a measured level)
Words: Reading time: about 2 minutes
Source: original passage written for practice, so full text is included.

Every few years, a film studio releases a new version of a story audiences already know. Critics sigh about a lack of imagination, yet the audiences keep coming. The habit is older than cinema: storytellers have retold the same tales for centuries, changing the setting, the speech and the ending to suit the listeners in front of them.

Familiarity is part of the appeal. A viewer who knows the outline of a story can relax into it and notice the details, such as which scenes were kept, which were cut and what the new version seems to think about them. A remake, at its best, is a conversation between two eras, with the second answering the first.

That does not excuse every retelling. Some copy the original so closely that they add nothing, and others change so much that the point of the story disappears. The test is not whether a work is new or old, but whether it has a reason to exist. A retelling that shows us something the original could not is worth making, however well known the plot.

Seen this way, the question is less "why do we keep retelling stories?" than "what is each retelling for?"
