The price of free shipping

Test ID: Topic: Editorial difficulty: (a guess before reading, not a measured level)
Words: Reading time: about 2 minutes
Source: original passage written for practice, so full text is included.

Online shops often offer free delivery on orders above a certain amount. The offer looks generous, and it works: a shopper who is ten dollars short of the limit will often add an item she did not plan to buy. The shop gains a larger sale, and the shopper feels clever for avoiding a delivery charge.

Yet the delivery was never free. Someone pays for the van, the fuel and the driver, and the cost is usually folded into the prices of the goods themselves. A person who orders a single small item may therefore be paying for the generosity shown to others without noticing. What looks like a gift is really a way of moving a cost from one place to another.

Economists are not troubled by this in itself. Prices must cover costs somewhere, and bundling can make buying simpler. Their concern is subtler: a charge that is hidden is a charge that cannot be compared. When delivery is added at the end, a shopper can see the full cost and decide. When it is buried in the price, she may compare only the numbers displayed and choose the dearer shop.

The practical advice is modest. Before adding that extra item to reach the limit, ask whether you would have bought it anyway. If not, the free delivery may be costing more than the fee it replaced.
