The explaining game

Test ID: Topic: Editorial difficulty: (a guess before reading, not a measured level)
Words: Reading time: about 2 minutes
Source: original passage written for practice, so full text is included.

Three years ago, Mira Sethi, who runs a small tuition centre in Keston, changed how her evening classes end. Before anyone opens a homework book, students pair up for what she calls the explaining game: each student must explain the day's main idea to a partner in their own words, and the partner must ask one question about it. The game takes ten loud minutes. Sethi says it is the best change she has ever made.

Her reason is simple. 'You cannot mumble an explanation,' she says. 'The moment you say an idea aloud, you find out whether you understand it or only nodded along.' She keeps a wall chart of the centre's average test scores, which rose from 61 to 74 over the three years since the game began.

Not everyone is convinced the game deserves the credit. Rohit Malhotra, whose son attends the centre, points out that Sethi also shortened the homework in the same year. He adds that the keenest students throw themselves into the game anyway, so the rise may reflect who the students are, and what else changed, rather than the explaining.

Sethi concedes she has not separated the causes, though she still believes the game helps. She also does not claim it suits every subject. When she tried it in the drawing class, where students learn by watching and copying, the explaining felt forced, and she dropped it after a month. 'Words help with words,' she says. 'A line is harder to talk about.'

The centre's owners now want the game in the younger children's classes as well. Sethi is hesitant. Younger children, she worries, may copy a partner's mistake with total confidence, and a wrong idea said aloud can be harder to forget. She has agreed to a trial with one class for a term, but not to a centre-wide roll-out. 'If it helps, we will know soon enough,' she says. 'If it only feels nice, I would rather know that too.'
