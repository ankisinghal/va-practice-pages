The card addressed to someone else

Test ID: Topic: Editorial difficulty: (a guess before reading, not a measured level)
Words: Reading time: about 2 minutes
Source: original passage written for practice, so full text is included.

At the community college's evening study room, Soren kept a stack of yellow cards. When a student arrived with a tangled decision, he asked the student to write the problem on one side and advice to an imagined stranger on the other. Kavya called it a gimmick. She wanted to leave a group project after one dismissive comment, but on her card she wrote, 'Say which part you can still finish before Friday.' Reading the sentence, she saw that the angry message she had drafted ignored her own advice.

Soren did not claim that the card supplied a stranger's wisdom. It changed the vantage point from which the first answer appeared. Advice offered to someone else was less wrapped in pride, embarrassment or the wish to win the last exchange. He called the exercise 'detached', not because the problem stopped mattering, but because the first answer was no longer wrapped in pride. The card could still mislead: if the student lacked information, distance might make a complicated choice sound easier than it was.

After two weeks, Soren told another tutor that the cards had made the students calmer, because fewer angry messages were being sent from the study room. The tutor was unconvinced. The mock examinations had ended during the same week, and students who had received plain blue cards with no instruction had also sent fewer messages. Kavya's own change was easier to describe. She had not become certain that staying was right; she had separated the leader's comment from the work she was willing to do.

At the next session, Kavya asked whether she should sign her card before returning it. Soren told her to leave it unsigned. She wrote one line on the back - 'Ask what you would tell someone you liked' - and put it in the stack for another student to find. The card did not settle her project, and it did not make Soren responsible for her choice. It borrowed enough distance to let her hear herself. When she picked up her books, the decision was still hers.
